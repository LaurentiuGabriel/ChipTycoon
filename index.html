ChipTycoon

Build the factory that turns sand into a computer chip

Your Park Map

Welcome, boss. This is your factory park. Trucks bring in plain sand at the gate. Twenty buildings later, tiny computer chips roll out the other side.

A chip is a small square of silicon with billions of tiny switches printed on it. Those switches do all the thinking in your phone, your laptop, your car and your washing machine. The whole trick is this: we draw the switches with light, one thin layer at a time, and we stack the layers up like a cake.

Yes, three months. A chip factory is the slowest ride in the park, and also the most expensive one. A single big factory costs more than a large airport.

The Whole Story in Six Lines
  • Sand is mostly silicon stuck to oxygen. We cook the oxygen off and keep the silicon.
  • We clean the silicon until it is unbelievably pure, then grow it into one giant crystal.
  • We slice the crystal into thin round plates called wafers, and polish them like mirrors.
  • We print a pattern onto the wafer with light, using a glass stencil called a mask.
  • We carve, add and repeat that printing about sixty times to build the switches and their wiring.
  • We test, cut and package the wafer into hundreds of finished chips, then ship them.

Every stage on the tour is one of those six ideas, just in more detail.

Before You Go In

Three words you will keep hearing

  • Wafer. The thin round plate of silicon. Think of a shiny grey pizza base about 30 cm across.
  • Die. One single chip on that wafer. A wafer holds anywhere from a few dozen to a few thousand dies.
  • Fab. Short for fabrication plant. It is the factory building where the printing happens.
