Word Book

Every term on this site, in one plain sentence

The Materials
Silica
The main stuff in quartz sand. It is silicon joined to oxygen.
Silicon
The grey element chips are made from. Not the same as silicone, which is the rubbery kitchen stuff.
Polysilicon
Very pure silicon that is still a jumble of small crystals. The starting lump for crystal growing.
Ingot
The big silver cylinder of silicon, grown as one single crystal, before it is sliced.
Wafer
A thin round plate cut from the ingot and polished like a mirror. Chips are built on top of it.
Doping
Deliberately adding a trace of another element to silicon so it conducts electricity the way we want.
Photoresist
A light sensitive liquid painted onto the wafer. Light changes it, so it can act as a stencil.
The Machines and Steps
Fab
Short for fabrication plant. The factory where wafers get printed and processed.
Cleanroom
A room with almost no dust in the air, because dust ruins chips.
Mask
A glass plate carrying the pattern for one layer of the chip. It works like a stencil for light.
Lithography
Printing the mask pattern onto the wafer with light. The single most important step.
EUV
Extreme ultraviolet. A very short wavelength light used to print the smallest patterns. Its machines use mirrors in a vacuum, because this light cannot pass through glass or air.
Develop
Washing away the parts of the photoresist that the light changed, leaving a stencil behind.
Etch
Eating away the material that the stencil left exposed, usually with a reactive gas.
Deposition
Adding a very thin layer of material on top of the wafer.
Ion implant
Firing doping atoms into the wafer at high speed so they land just under the surface.
Interconnect
The stacked copper wiring that joins all the transistors together.
Planarisation
Polishing the wafer flat again after a layer is built, so the next layer has a level surface to sit on.
The Product
Transistor
A tiny electrical switch. Billions of them wired together are what make a chip able to compute.
Gate
The control terminal on a transistor. Put voltage on it and the switch turns on.
Die
One individual chip, as cut from the wafer, before it gets a case.
Package
The protective case around the die, with contacts so it can be soldered onto a circuit board.
Yield
The share of chips on a wafer that actually work. High yield means profit, low yield means trouble.
Node
The name given to a generation of chip making, such as 5 nm or 3 nm. These days it is mostly a marketing label rather than a real measurement.
Nanometre
A millionth of a millimetre. A human hair is roughly 80,000 nanometres wide.
Binning
Sorting finished chips by how fast and reliable they turned out, then selling them as different models.
Common Mix Ups
  • Chip and processor are not the same thing. A processor is one kind of chip. Memory, sensors and power controllers are chips too.
  • The black rectangle is not the chip. That is the package. The real chip inside is much smaller and is bare silicon.
  • Chips are not assembled from parts. Nothing is placed one by one. Everything is printed and carved all at once, across the whole wafer.
  • Smaller is not automatically better. A smaller pattern means more switches in the same space, but it also means more cost, more heat problems and lower yield.
